# Prior Art Registry

A structured record of prior art found during searches, organized by IP Tracker entry. Each entry documents what was found, how it relates, and how the invention distinguishes from it.

**IMPORTANT: This document is for ASSESSMENT only. Teachings from prior art must NOT be integrated into the inventions. See the Intellectual Firewall section below.**

---

## Intellectual Firewall

Prior art is searched to assess novelty and inform claim strategy — NOT to improve or modify the invention. The following rules are absolute:

- **Never suggest features, improvements, or modifications to an invention based on what was found in prior art.** If a patent describes an interesting technique, do not propose adding it to the user's system.
- **Never incorporate terminology from prior art into the invention's description.** The invention should be described in the inventor's own words, not in language borrowed from prior art references.
- **Never suggest "combining" the invention with a prior art approach.** The invention is what the inventor built. Prior art is what others built. They stay separate.
- **Assessment is the only purpose.** Prior art answers: "Is this novel?" and "How do we frame claims to distinguish from this?" It does not answer: "How could we make this better?"

If a prior art search reveals a genuinely better approach than what the inventor built, the correct response is to note the distinction — not to suggest the inventor adopt the prior art approach. The inventor may independently arrive at a similar improvement through their own reasoning, and that's fine. But Claude Code must not be the conduit.
